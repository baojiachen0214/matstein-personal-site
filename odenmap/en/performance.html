TEST NOTES

Numbers, with their context.

These results describe specific development checks, not an independent benchmark or a promise for every device and document. Resources, text complexity, zoom level and visible node count affect the experience.

Internal test records: October 9–12, 2026

20,000-level structural checks

A chain with 20,000 levels was checked for finite coordinates, complete node counts, branch moves, structure changes and undo/redo equivalence. The October 12 record contains 20,011 structural assertions. A separate native-window check verified that focusing far down the chain and reversing the layout retained visible nodes.

This does not mean 20,000 nodes render smoothly at once. A dense full-map view still has noticeable delays.

Long-topic loading: about 52% less time

On an Apple M5 Pro with 24 GiB memory and macOS 27.0.1, optimized test binaries used identical test scheduling and three independent before/after runs, taking medians. A long-topic loading scenario in a 1,000-node document with 5,000 source paragraphs changed from 1,344.653 ms to 642.035 ms, a reduction of approximately 52.3% versus the earlier implementation. It is a local loading-call measurement, not key-to-screen latency.

In a separate 10,000-node / 5,000-paragraph scenario, combined store initialization and native editor creation changed from 1,924.269 ms to 1,105.765 ms, approximately 42.5% less time. First editing and paragraph splitting at this text size still exceed a 10 ms target.

Dense drawing remains a work in progress

The October 12 CPU raster-output test used a 1,200 × 800 non-Retina canvas, 20 frames and four warm-up frames. It includes background, grid, drawing and ImageRenderer output; it is not physical-screen FPS or touch-event latency.
